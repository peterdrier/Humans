# Tickets — Section Invariants

External ticket vendor sync (orders + attendees), Stripe-fee enrichment, auto-matching to humans by email, event-participation derivation, and 2027 ticketing, where Humans sells tickets itself.

## Purpose

Tickets answers who may come to the event, what they paid, and how the money reconciles. Up to 2026 the answer came from Ticket Tailor and this section mirrored it. From 2027 Humans sells the tickets itself: it decides who may buy which ticket at what price, owns the order and the named ticket, and lets a ticketing platform (Box Office on Stripe, pretix or Hi.Events) do nothing but take the money and refund it. The platforms are interchangeable behind one port, so the collective can switch platform without moving its rules.

**What good looks like.** A member buys at their own price in a few clicks and gets a ticket with a QR code. Every ticket names a person, and the gate admits exactly the tickets Humans issued and still holds valid. The ticketing team can find any order, see its whole history, refund it and run the resale queue without opening the platform's dashboard. Every cent our side recorded matches what the platform says it charged, refunded, lost to chargebacks, kept as fees and paid out, per period, and a difference can be drilled into.

**Deliberate choices.** Prices are computed in Humans and sent as exact amounts; a platform never discounts or adds a fee. Tickets are named and never renamed by a holder; where resale is on, a ticket is resold through Humans' resale queue, which voids it and issues a new one. The ticketing team may correct a holder's legal name or, as the exception, move a full price ticket to another person; moving a class priced ticket is an Admin override. A signed-out buyer signs in before paying; there is no guest checkout. Platform connection settings are server configuration, never stored in Humans; the per-event platform choice is part of the event's ticketing settings. Every order step lands on an append-only timeline. Schemes another organisation running Humans may not use are per-event switches in the ticketing settings (`TicketingSwitches`, #2519): resale (`Off` / `Queue` / `QueueAndDirected`), tickets for others, donations, the mailing-list opt-in and companion tickets, enforced at checkout, not only hidden. Which child ticket types and donation purposes exist is per-event configuration too (A29, checkout.md "Ticket types per event"). Beside them sit mode (`Closed` / `InvitedOnly` / `Open` / `ResaleOnly`); resale itself opens by hand on the resale queue page (`ResaleOpenedAt`), never on a threshold, and a directed resale link has no expiry.

**Out of scope.** Signing anything at checkout or at the gate, and any use of Consent for ticketing (Peter, 2026-11-21). Manual IBAN payment. Discount codes in Humans' own checkout (the vendor mirror still mints them for Campaigns). Orders that bypass Humans' price rule. The admission record itself, which is Gate's scan row.

Dashboard statistics, break-even budget and vendor-capacity reads honor request cancellation through the page builder and service decorator. Requested cancellation propagates; an unrelated vendor-capacity failure retains the zero-capacity fallback. The local stale-sync recovery save uses the same request token.

Checkout methods that write to a ticketing platform carry `[ExternalWrite]`. Buyer cancel, admin refund, ticket void and the BoxOffice, Hi.Events and pretix webhooks call them with `CancellationToken.None` after the actor lookup or signature check. Buyer/holder and admin order GETs read platform status with the request token: they update local totals and fulfil newly observed payments, but never cancel platform orders or replay refunds. Paid donation-only orders enqueue the existing buyer receipt without tickets or holder invitations. Recovery includes missing donation receipts in the same 30-minute grace and seven-day retry window, stopping at the delivery timeline marker. Payment-method changes persist even when state, fee and money totals are unchanged. Webhooks and background reconciliation recover lapsed orders and settle pending refunds. Pretix timeouts, rate limits, unknown refund states and failed refund-fee cleanup retain a pending intent until retry resolves them. Refund recovery persists changed diagnostics even when state and platform ID stay the same. It bounds failure text to 1,000 characters without splitting surrogate pairs, clears stale errors and manual-payment links on success, and records identical normalized outcomes only once. Every platform refund attempt carries the original persisted request time, including retries and named-refund settlement; BoxOffice refuses unseen refunds outside Stripe's 24-hour retry window and keeps them pending for reconciliation. Automated outcomes are resolved first; their succeeded or unresolved amounts are reserved before manual refunds settle oldest-first from the remaining refunded total. An open order retains its reservation until platform cancellation is confirmed. Directed resale invitation retries use stable delivery IDs and authenticated invitation identities, preserving already-mailed links without storing raw tokens. New pending deliveries can be retried without consuming another lifetime allowance; completed addresses are skipped. Legacy hash links remain valid. Cancellation and erasure invalidate every encoding by removing its invitation record, and pending addresses appear in the GDPR export. The sales-close instant also closes new resale offers and invitations, including at the exact boundary. Member controls, directed links, stock selection and the admin selling status use that same snapshot-time rule. A holder may still offer while resale is paused before closing; invitations wait until resale is selling. Offer creation saves the new offer and the ticket’s `Offered` state together and refuses another open or reserved offer for that ticket. Checkout and directed-link availability require one unambiguous active offer for an offered adult ticket; invalid or ambiguous rows remain untouched for operator review. Eligible queue ordering stays oldest-first. Holder, staff and resale-switch withdrawals save the offer and the returned ticket in one repository commit. Reconciliation finishes open-offer withdrawals when resale is off; it returns only `Offered` tickets to `Valid`, preserving retired states and pending buyer reservations. Ticket renames update the exact order line identified by the ticket’s immutable `LineIndex`, saving both records in one repository commit. A missing or non-adult ticket line refuses the rename before account resolution; merged holders cannot redirect the update to another line. A refunded ticket void persists its original amount, reason and refund ID before contacting the platform. An interrupted request resumes that intent, reuses the refund and blocks rename or resale until completion. Ticket, valid dependents and offer withdrawal commit together; a declined refund clears the intent and keeps the tickets usable. Pending void details are exported and erased with the holder. Checkout export and erasure share ownership discovery by account ID and known addresses, matching emails only for unbound holders. They include dependent tickets and order lines across orders, erase platform copies before local mutation and void every matched live barcode; another buyer's identity and totals stay outside a holder's export. Restoration refuses erased ticket identity markers and missing or tombstoned holders before mutation, timeline or audit; a live merge survivor remains eligible through Users’ canonical lookup. Companion issuance saves its zero-total order and attached ticket in one repository commit. An unaddressed companion receives its QR through its live adult holder even on a separate order. Adult initial/resend and renamed-ticket mail include valid unaddressed dependents across the event; profile cards likewise find attached tickets across orders, while public profiles remain empty. Receipts and follow-up invitations stay scoped to the delivered order. Direct staff issuance also refuses a companion whose unissued purchase still holds capacity, including purchases made for them by another buyer; expired local and definitively closed orders do not block issuance. Approval can complete a pending request from an already-issued valid companion with the same parent and holder, without issuing or delivering a duplicate. Resale sold notices persist a pending intent with the sale and retry until the email outbox accepts the notice and its audit is recorded. Unsold notices likewise remain pending until outbox acceptance. Stable delivery IDs dedupe retries; legacy sold offers with no intent are not backfilled. The sold intent is exported and cleared on holder erasure. Resale retirement saves the adult and its valid children and companions together. Gate lookup and event lists refuse a dependent whose parent is missing, void or resold, including older partial retirements awaiting recovery. Sales closing releases admission only when no reserved buyer order still holds the ticket; overdue remote, processing, paid and indeterminate reservations block the adult and its dependents until resolved. A sold offer invalidates them even before retirement recovery finishes. Resale admission checks include valid children and companions across the event, including dependents on separate orders, before offering, inviting or accepting a directed checkout. Payment fulfilment rechecks that family before selling: an admitted family keeps its tickets, its offer is withdrawn atomically, and the buyer receives fresh stock with an attention audit; no seller refund or sold notice is created. Capacity and holder checks retain paid orders until issuance succeeds, without counting issued orders twice or restoring holds for their retired tickets. Fulfilment excludes its own order from duplicate-holder detection. Capacity and holder checks retain overdue remote orders until an explicit closing state; the buy-page order link and resale diagnostics use the same rule. Only an unaccepted local order lapses by the clock. Buyer cancellation also retains an open remote order and its resale reservation if its platform adapter is missing, returning a localized refusal; an order without a remote ID may still be cancelled locally. A started recovery cancellation and its local outcome finish even during job shutdown.

Bulk cache warmups publish through the shared mutation guard. A refresh, deletion, eviction or clear overlapping the load discards the partial attempt and reloads the full snapshot and derived indexes before a read can treat the cache as warmed.

Configured ticket rules must be available before checkout. Successful GitHub reads are cached for ten minutes;
a failed refresh replaces the cached rules with an unavailable result for one minute. Buy pages and checkout
report `Tickets_Buy_TermsUnavailable` rather than accepting an older rules version.

Support-page ordering belongs to `TicketsSupportAdminController`: newest refunds first, waiting resale offers by queue
position followed by newest closed offers, and report tables by count/label, purpose or state. The report calculator
retains queue-position calculation and paid-day chronology for running totals.

Raw checkout order, holder-ticket, event-ticket, offer and refund reads return repository rows without presentation
ordering. The admin order list sorts newest first; lookup tickets and dependent-ticket panels sort by issue time.
Order-detail and delivery-card presentation ordering belongs to their rendering callers.

Operator controllers order companion requests pending-first/newest-first, cancellation refund groups by state,
and backend lists by key. Backend status pages order orders and stuck orders newest-first and refunds oldest-first;
the reconciliation start date is the minimum order timestamp in the organisation's zone, independent of row order.
Cancellation processing still uses oldest-paid-first work order.

Buy-page read models carry the event's gate-opening date. `TicketBuyController` orders public and signed-in sales
by that date and each normal or directed buy page's order history newest-first; service reads do not choose those
presentation orders.

Ticket families on “My tickets” are ordered in the controller by the holder ticket's issue time within each event,
with dependents beside that holder. Order pages and profile child lists choose their own kind/issue-time ordering;
admin refund rows are oldest-first. Email rendering keeps the recipient card first and orders dependent cards by
kind and issue time. Checkout reads supply rows without these presentation sorts; audit streams and FIFO
allocation, settlement and cancellation processing retain their domain ordering.

## Concepts

- **Ticket Orders** and **Ticket Attendees** are records synced from an external ticket vendor (Ticket Tailor in production, an in-process stub in dev). They are not manually created in the system.
- A **Ticket Order** represents a purchase (one per transaction). It carries the gross total, currency, vendor discount/donation line-item amounts, computed VAT (using VIP-split logic, not the vendor's tax line), and is enriched with Stripe fee data (payment method, Stripe fee, application fee, fee VAT, withheld tax) during sync via `IStripeService.GetPaymentDetailsAsync` keyed by the vendor's payment-intent id.
- A **Ticket Attendee** represents an individual ticket holder (one per issued ticket, multiple per order). Tickets above `TicketConstants.VipThresholdEuros` (315 EUR) are treated as VIP — the portion above the threshold is a VAT-free donation, the portion at-or-below is taxable at `TicketConstants.VatRate` (10%) inclusive.
- **Auto-matching** links orders to humans by buyer email and attendees to humans by attendee email. The lookup runs only against **verified** `UserEmails` rows under `NormalizingEmailComparer` so gmail/googlemail aliases collapse. A normalized verified email is supposed to be owned by exactly one user — if a normalized email maps to multiple verified users (data-integrity error, should not happen), the email is left unmatched and a `LogError` is emitted. Unverified emails never participate in matching.
- **Ticket Sync** is a background Hangfire job (`TicketSyncJob`, interval from `TicketVendor:SyncIntervalMinutes` — default 15, `appsettings.json` sets 5) that pulls order, attendee, and gate check-in data from the vendor through `ITicketVendorService`. The scheduled run only syncs while **Scheduled sync** is on at `/Settings#tickets` (off by default, for between sales seasons); the admin **Sync now** and **Full resync** buttons run regardless.
- **TicketTransferRequest** is a retired 2026 record: a member asked the ticket team to move a TicketTailor ticket to another Humans member. The request pages, service and emails are gone and nothing creates or changes a request; directed resale replaces them. The `ticket_transfer_requests` table and its 2026 rows stay until the old system is cleaned up: approved rows still name who a voided ticket went to, and account merge and GDPR erasure still reach every row.
- **Vendor connector** is a thin adapter (`Humans.TicketTailor`) behind `ITicketVendorService`. Production binds `TicketTailorService` (HTTP client against `https://api.tickettailor.com/v1`); non-production binds `StubTicketVendorService` (deterministic in-memory fixture with ~450 orders / ~600 tickets).
- **Attendee Contact Import** is a manually-triggered admin job (`IAttendeeContactImportService`) that creates a no-profile Humans user for each unmatched ticket attendee whose email doesn't already resolve to an existing UserEmail. Mirrors the MailerLite import's plan/apply shape with squatter protection (Users atomically replaces an unverified row and provisions the new verified owner, preserving the original row if provisioning fails). Runs only when an admin triggers it; the sync does not invoke it.

- **Selling in Humans** (2027 ticketing; spec [features/checkout.md](features/checkout.md)): Humans sells tickets itself. Tickets owns the price rule, the orders and the named ticket lines, and routes each order to the event's **ticketing platform**, an adapter section implementing `ITicketingPlatform` (Contracts leaf): `boxoffice` (Stripe direct, `Humans.BoxOffice`), `pretix`, `hievents`. A platform only charges the lines it is given, reports the outcome and refunds an exact amount. Connection settings (instance URL, keys, secrets) are each adapter's server configuration, never Tickets' tables (Peter, 2026-11-21); each adapter describes them through the port's `Setup` and tests them with `CheckAsync`, and the per-event platform choice is part of the event's ticketing settings. Its webhook is a nudge to `ITicketingPlatformEvents`; Tickets always re-reads the platform's status, and `TicketCheckoutReconcileJob` polls open orders every 5 minutes for missed webhooks. **Reconciliation** compares Humans' orders on each platform with the platform's own list per period (see checkout.md); `TicketReconJob` runs it daily over the whole sale, with each platform's Stripe fees and payouts, and mails the ticketing team what does not balance.
- **Ticketing settings** (`TicketingSettings`) are one Settings event's ticketing configuration: platform key, the event's id inside the platform, mode (`Closed` / `InvitedOnly` / `Open` / `ResaleOnly`), sales close instant, capacity, ticket VAT, dated **waves**, **VIP totals** (`VipTotalsMinor`, the VIP ticket buttons) and **price rules** mapping a Grants program to a class price, a discount, a wave pin or early access. `InvitedOnly` is early sales: only people with an `EarlyAccess` grant buy, fresh stock only, with the rule's amount off while they do (the largest one when several apply, never the sum); no tickets for others, no resale, nothing on the public page, and ticketing settings in this mode need at least one `EarlyAccess` rule.
- **Price rule:** wave price on the pricing date (today in the event's zone; the last wave holds until sales close), replaced by the cheapest class price among the buyer's active grants, minus every discount, floored at 0. A partner's ticket always costs the public price. A VIP ticket is the buyer's ticket plus the rest of its total as a VAT-free donation line; a free donation with a purpose may be added or bought alone.
- An **order** (`TicketCheckoutOrder`) is everything one buyer pays for at once: buyer, platform key and id, state (`Created` → `Pending` → (`Processing` →) `Paid` | `Failed` | `Expired` | `Cancelled`; `Processing` is a payment on its way, which holds the tickets and cannot be cancelled), total, fee, method, refunded and charged-back totals, and its lines (ticket lines carry the holder's legal name, email, user id, pricing facts and, for children, the adult line they ride on). A paid order never leaves `Paid` and issues its tickets once.
- A **ticket** (`TicketCheckoutTicket`) is what the gate scans: one per paid ticket line, unique barcode, `Valid` → `Offered` (in the resale queue; refused at the gate while it can still sell) → `Resold` | `Void`. Never renamed by a holder; resale voids it and issues a new one. Gate reads it through `ITicketGateLookup`; Tickets reads Gate's `IGateAdmissions` so a used ticket is never offered or sold. See checkout.md, At the gate.
- A **Resale Offer** is a holder's ticket waiting in the queue, oldest first; it may be directed to up to ten invited emails, each with its own link, and the first to buy gets it. The holder's refund goes out in the evening batch once the buyer's order has held. A zero-total order is paid at once without a platform.
- **Ticket delivery** (spec [features/ticket-delivery.md](features/ticket-delivery.md)): once a paid order's tickets are issued, `ITicketDelivery` queues one ticket email per named holder (QR of the ticket's barcode, holder name, kind, ticket rules), one receipt to the buyer (ticket lines with their VAT, donations without), and schedules a volunteer invitation to each holder two days later. Members see their tickets with QR codes at `/Tickets/Buy/Mine` and on their profile sidebar.

## Data Model

### TicketingSettings

**Table:** `ticket_sales_events` — one per Settings event (`EventSettingsId` unique). `Waves`, `PriceRules` and `ChildTypes` (name, under age, price) are jsonb; `VipTotalsMinor` is `bigint[]` and `DonationPurposes` is `text[]`. `Switches` (jsonb, nullable; null reads as the defaults) holds the event's ticketing switches: resale scheme (`Off`, `Queue`, `QueueAndDirected`), tickets for others (`PartnerTickets`), donations, mailing list opt-in, companion tickets (`CompanionTickets`, null reads as on) (see checkout.md). Amounts are minor units (cents) throughout 2027 ticketing. The ticketing settings editor reads prices and VAT in invariant HTML-number format, accepting exponent notation and rejecting grouping separators. It rounds euro inputs to cents away from zero and refuses values outside the signed 64-bit minor-unit range with the existing field error.

### TicketCheckoutOrder

**Table:** `ticket_checkout_orders` — `Lines` is jsonb. Unique (`PlatformKey`, `PlatformOrderId`). `TermsFolder` and `TermsVersion` (a legal repo commit) record the ticket rules the buyer accepted. `CancellationEmailedAt` marks the buyer emailed after an event cancellation. `BuyerUserId` and each line's `HolderUserId` (stored as `AttendeeUserId` in the jsonb) are bare user ids (no FK).

### TicketCheckoutRefund

**Table:** `ticket_checkout_refunds` — one row per refund request, saved before the platform call so a crash still counts it; `Id` is the platform's idempotency key.

### TicketCheckoutTicket

**Table:** `ticket_checkout_tickets` — unique `Barcode`; unique (`OrderId`, `LineIndex`), so a paid order issues each line once; indexes on `TicketingSettingsId`, `HolderUserId`, `ParentTicketId`. `ParentTicketId` links a child or companion to its adult. Erasure tombstones `HolderName` and `HolderEmail`, children's names included.

### TicketResaleOffer

**Table:** `ticket_resale_offers` — one per time a ticket goes into resale (`Open` → `Reserved` → `Sold`, or `Withdrawn`). `directed_invites` (jsonb) lists each invited email with the SHA-256 of its link's token; the token is never stored. `DirectedInviteCount`, summed over a ticket's offers, caps its invitations at ten. `RefundId` is set by the evening refund batch; `UnsoldNotifiedAt` once the holder is told it did not sell. Erasure clears every invitation on the erased holder's offers and their own address from anyone else's.

### TicketCompanionRequest

**Table:** `ticket_companion_requests` — a holder's ask for a free companion ticket for someone already in Humans (`Pending` → `Approved` / `Declined`, or `Withdrawn`). `Note` is the holder's reason, `DecisionNote` the team's (required to decline). Approval issues the companion ticket to the named account (`CompanionTicketId`); no account is ever created. Erasure withdraws a pending request and tombstones `Note` where the member is holder or companion; merge reassigns holder, companion and decider.

### TicketCheckoutOrderEvent

**Table:** `ticket_checkout_order_events` — the order's timeline, append-only (the repository has no update or delete): created, checkout opened, platform refused, state changed, refunded total changed, refund requested and settled, cancelled. `ActorUserId` is the person, null for automation. `Detail` holds amounts, states and platform refs, never names or emails, so erasure leaves it; merge reassigns `ActorUserId`. Checkout timeline details and bounded reasons truncate without splitting a Unicode surrogate pair.

### TicketOrder

**Table:** `ticket_orders`

Ticket purchase order synced from vendor (one per purchase). Vendor-agnostic identity is `VendorOrderId`; payment-intent linkage is `StripePaymentIntentId` (from the vendor's `txn_id`). Stripe enrichment fields (`PaymentMethod`, `PaymentMethodDetail`, `StripeFee`, `ApplicationFee`, `FeeTax`, `WithheldTax`) are filled in by `EnrichOrdersWithStripeDataAsync` after the upsert and preserved across re-syncs. `DonationAmount` (standalone vendor donation line items, VAT-exempt) and `DiscountAmount` (absolute value of vendor `gift_card` line items) come from the vendor; `VatAmount` is recomputed locally via VIP-split logic — the vendor's tax line is intentionally ignored because Ticket Tailor mis-applies 10% to the full ticket price.

Cross-domain nav `TicketOrder.MatchedUser` stripped (FK `MatchedUserId` retained; nav property removed). No FK-join in `ITicketRepository`; the order and attendee grids carry only `MatchedUserId` and render the current name through `<vc:human>`, without a service-side name lookup.
Aggregate-local: `TicketOrder.Attendees`.

### TicketAttendee

**Table:** `ticket_attendees`

Individual ticket holder (issued ticket, multiple per order). Vendor-agnostic identity is `VendorTicketId`. `AttendeeEmail` is nullable — some vendor flows don't capture per-ticket email; in that case `MatchedUserId` stays null. `Status` is normalized from the vendor string into `TicketAttendeeStatus` (`Valid` / `CheckedIn` / `Void`). Only `Valid` and `CheckedIn` count as revenue or as ticket coverage. `Barcode` is the Ticket Tailor `issued_ticket.barcode` value (the code printed on the ticket and encoded in its QR), nullable, lazy-filled during sync; a Full Re-sync backfills existing rows. It is indexed and searchable (the `/Tickets/Attendees` search matches barcode alongside name and email). Ticket Tailor gives each ticket two identities — the object id `ti_…` (`VendorTicketId`) and the short scannable `barcode` — and offers **no lookup-by-barcode endpoint** (retrieve is by `ti_…`; list filters by event/time/cursor only). Every barcode→ticket resolution therefore runs against our own synced rows and the cached `TicketOrderInfo` projection, never a live vendor call. `CheckedInAt` is the gate-scan time sourced from the vendor's check-in resource (TicketTailor `/check_ins`) during sync — orthogonal to `Status` (a scanned ticket stays `Valid`; check-in is a separate vendor concept), write-once per ticket (earliest scan wins, never cleared by later syncs), null until checked in.

Cross-domain nav `TicketAttendee.MatchedUser` stripped (FK `MatchedUserId` retained; nav property removed). No FK-join in `ITicketRepository`; the order and attendee grids carry only `MatchedUserId` and render the current name through `<vc:human>`, without a service-side name lookup.
Aggregate-local: `TicketAttendee.TicketOrder`.

`AttendeeName` accepts up to 513 characters, matching the retired transfer's full legal-name snapshot.

The Codes and Humans & Tickets display rows carry recipient/user IDs for `<vc:human>`, without copied member names. Name search runs against the upstream campaign facts or private report rows before projecting the display rows.

**Barcode lookup:** `ITicketService.FindCurrentEventAttendeeByBarcodeAsync` owns the shared Teams/Scanner lookup: trim input, reject blank codes, restrict to current-event orders and compare ordinally. It returns the first matching attendee regardless of status (including void transfer cards). The caching decorator uses its existing order snapshot; the inner service applies the same rule to its projection.

**`TicketAttendeeInfo` read projection** (part of `TicketOrderInfo`): carries `Barcode` and `CheckedInAt` plus, for `Void` attendees, `TransferredToName` and `TransferredAt` sourced from the Approved `TicketTransferRequest` for the attendee (read via `ITicketTransferRepository` in `TicketQueryService.GetTicketOrdersAsync`). These fields are used by the Scanner ticket-lookup card (`/Scanner/Tickets`).

### TicketSyncState

**Table:** `ticket_sync_states`

Singleton (`Id` always 1) tracking ticket sync operational state. `VendorEventId` records the event currently being synced. `LastSyncAt` doubles as the resume cursor passed to the vendor's `updated_at.gte` filter on the next run. `SyncStatus` is `Idle` / `Running` / `Error`; if a sync is found stuck in `Running` for >30 min, `GetDashboardStatsAsync` auto-resets it to `Error` with a stale-state message. `FullResync` clears `LastSyncAt` so the next run pulls all orders again.

### TicketSettings

**Table:** `ticket_settings`

Singleton (`Id` always 1, seeded) of admin-editable section settings, edited on `/Settings#tickets` (TicketsPolicies.Manage). `IsScheduledSyncEnabled` gates the recurring `TicketSyncJob`; each change is audited as `TicketScheduledSyncEnabled` / `TicketScheduledSyncDisabled`.

### TicketTransferRequest

**Table:** `ticket_transfer_requests`

Retired 2026 transfer request (no writer). `OriginalTicketAttendeeId` FK → `ticket_attendees`. `SenderUserId` / `ReceiverUserId` / `DecidedByUserId` → users. `ReceiverLegalName` / `ReceiverEmail` are the request-time receiver snapshot. `Status` is `TicketTransferStatus` (`Pending` / `Approved` / `Rejected` / `Cancelled`); only `Approved` rows are read, for the void attendee's `TransferredToName` / `TransferredAt`. `SenderReason` and `AdminNotes` are free text. `VendorResult` / `VendorMessage` / `NewVendorTicketId` / `VendorHoldId` / `VendorStepsJson` held the TicketTailor void+reissue outcome and are unread. The table is kept, not dropped, while it holds 2026 data.

**Indexes:** `(SenderUserId, Status)` and `Status`, from when the pages existed.

**Cross-section FKs:** `SenderUserId`, `ReceiverUserId`, `DecidedByUserId` → Users (FK only, no nav).

### EventParticipation

**Table:** `ticket_event_participations`

One record per `(UserId, Year)` (unique index; no FK). `Status` is `ParticipationStatus` (`NotAttending` / `Ticketed` / `Attended` / `NoShow`), stored as string, nullable: historical cleared rows have null status and read as unknown; new removals delete the row. `Source` is `ParticipationSource` (`UserDeclared` / `TicketSync` / `AdminBackfill`). `DeclaredAt` stamps a self-declaration; `CheckedInAt` is the first gate check-in (write-once). `ParticipationStatus` / `ParticipationSource` live in `Humans.Tickets.Contracts`.

Lifecycle: `Attended` is permanent (only a missing `CheckedInAt` may be filled); a clear needs a matching `Source` and never touches an `Attended` record. Ticket sync derives the `TicketSync` rows (Triggers below); the member declares `NotAttending` (`UserDeclared`); the admin backfill writes `AdminBackfill`.

## Invariants

- Ticketing adapters classify Stripe reporting categories through `PaymentLedgerEntry.KindOf` on the existing ledger contract. Stripe reporting rejects ledger amounts without explicit EUR; unverified fee currencies stay unknown instead of being treated as EUR cents. All three adapters reject native status and order-list amounts without explicit EUR; pretix confirms the parent event's currency live before reading order money.

- Ticketing settings require explicit event, platform, sales mode, capacity, VAT, resale scheme, checkout switches and each posted price-rule kind. Binding errors return 400 with a Warning after actor resolution and before conversion or saving; missing values never apply form defaults. Explicit zero/false values, optional text, wholly blank spare rows and disabled wave-pin amounts retain their existing meanings.
- Admin refund and ticket-void forms reuse the bounded invariant-culture euro parser used for ticket prices. The parser accepts invariant dot decimals and exponent notation, rejecting grouping separators rather than treating a comma decimal as a larger refund. Amounts round to cents away from zero before range checking and conversion, so malformed, nonpositive or unrepresentable refunds never reach checkout. Binding failures return 400 with a Warning; semantic amount refusals warn and show operator feedback. Refund requires an amount and voiding requires an explicit refund/no-refund choice. Explicit no-refund voids ignore the unused amount. The service still enforces the order's refundable cap.
- Resale open/pause and companion decisions require an explicit true/false command. These actions and the scheduled-sync setting return 400 with a Warning before mutation when binding fails. A missing scheduled-sync checkbox still means off; missing resale or companion commands never become an implicit pause or decline.
- Buyer checkout rejects binding errors before creating an order. Donations use the HTML number field's invariant dot-decimal format (including exponent notation) in every request culture, without grouping separators; invalid or unrepresentable amounts refuse checkout instead of becoming zero. Blank or missing donations remain zero, nonpositive amounts retain their zero behavior, the existing donation cap applies before conversion, and cents round away from zero. Optional checkboxes, friend addresses and paired child fields retain their form semantics.

- Checkout refunds observe cancellation before an attempt starts, then finish the platform mutation, saved outcome, timeline and order refresh independently of request or job cancellation. Initial status reads retain the caller token. Settlement replays use the same protected mutation boundary and stop before the next refund when cancellation is requested.

- Single-row selection uses the newest approved transfer decision per attendee, the oldest backend order when none is recorded, and the cheapest eligible class price. Direct extrema selectors retain the first source row on equal keys.

- Section-owned resource keys use the `Tickets_` prefix in all supported cultures.
- The gate credential page links to the Gate admissions terminal and explains that the shared account can record admissions. The kiosk is restricted to Gate and its sign-in/sign-out pages; it cannot browse Scanner or ticket administration.

- Shared table currency and number cells use the selected UI culture; numeric sort values stay invariant. Guest order totals, dashboard cards, order and attendee amounts, VIP splits, fee rates and sales aggregate totals use the same UI culture; chart JSON stays numeric.
- Orders, Attendees and Who Hasn’t Bought GETs retain request cancellation through ticket queries, ticket-type filters and member/team/settings enrichment. Code tracking, sales aggregates and the accountant CSV retain it through reporting queries and campaign enrichment.
- The Codes controller orders campaign summaries newest first. Sales campaign rows retain unattributed-last, discount-descending and title ordering, with campaign creation time descending as the final tie-breaker. Campaigns supplies creation metadata; report services do not impose these display orders.
- Ticket vendor health probes propagate requested timeout/abort cancellation without logging a vendor timeout. A vendor timeout while the caller token remains active still returns Degraded.
- The member dashboard's ticket card (`MemberTicketStatusViewComponent`) renders nothing while the active event is off season (the admin switch on the active event settings) and no event is on sale. When the member's holdings fail to load it shows a "ticket status unavailable" note — never "no ticket" with the buy/declare actions. While any event's sales mode is not Closed it offers the Humans buy page (`/Tickets/Buy`) instead of the ticket vendor's shop link, whether or not the ticket vendor sync is configured. Checkout owns its Humans-issued ticket count: held tickets for the active event and any unclosed sale, or every event when none is active. `TicketCheckoutTicket.IsHeld` means Valid or Offered and is shared by buy eligibility, stock, the holder list, delivery, support reports, cancellation, erasure and deletion holds; gate admission keeps its separate resale guard. The card adds that count to the vendor sync's, so a Humans buyer sees their ticket and a link to `/Tickets/Buy/Mine`.
- `OnsiteChipViewComponent` ("Onsite since {time}", nobodies-collective/Humans#736) is contributed to Users' `UserPartSlots.Profile` slot. It renders for the member on their own profile (`ProfileCardViewMode.Self`, inside their ticket holdings card rather than as its own part) or a viewer passing `TicketsPolicies.Read`, and only when the active event year's participation is `Attended` with a check-in. Nothing for anyone else.
- Participation reads return the section's rows without year ordering. Year-specific lookups, sets and counts do not depend on list order; the GDPR export projection owns ascending year order.
- `TicketHoldingsViewComponent` is contributed (`Humans.Tickets/Section.cs`, `IUserPart`) to Users' `user-profile-sidebar` and `user-admin-detail-sidebar` slots. It renders nothing for `ProfileCardViewMode.Public` — its only visibility check — and shows an empty-holdings card only for `Admin`.
- `TicketOrderInfo.IsForEvent(..., paidOnly: true)` owns paid-order selection for Budget actuals and Finance cash flow: only Paid orders count; a configured vendor event id matches ordinally, including when that event is no longer current; null selects `IsCurrentEvent`. Its default includes every payment status, so Budget can distinguish missing event data from an event whose orders were all refunded.
- Order and attendee grid search is a case-insensitive literal substring match, including regex characters in names, emails, discount codes and barcodes. Escaped matching stays in the database before counting and paging; InMemory uses explicit invariant matching. Duplicate-ticket email grouping uses `OrdinalIgnoreCase` over the bounded unmatched-attendee identity projection; matched humans remain grouped by user id.
- Ticket orders and attendees are synced from the external vendor — they cannot be manually created or edited from this app.
- Stripe enrichment (`PaymentMethod`, `PaymentMethodDetail`, `StripeFee`, `ApplicationFee`, `FeeTax`, `WithheldTax`) is preserved across re-syncs and only re-run for orders that have a `StripePaymentIntentId` and are still missing fee data (`StripeFee` null); if `IStripeService.IsConfigured` is false the pass is silently skipped. A charge awaiting its asynchronous balance transaction leaves fees null so a later sync retries, rather than permanently recording zero fees.
- Stripe fees enter the EUR reports only when their balance transaction and fee entries
  explicitly declare EUR. A currency refusal uses the existing per-order Warning and leaves
  missing fees eligible for retry; no foreign amounts are relabeled or converted.
- `FeeTax` (Stripe's `tax` bucket: VAT on its fees) and `WithheldTax` (Stripe's `withheld_tax`) are kept apart from `StripeFee`, never folded into it. Both are money Stripe keeps, so dashboard `NetRevenue` deducts them with the fees. The Net Revenue card's breakdown line, the monthly recap and both CSV exports show them as separate figures; the per-payment-method fee breakdown (and its effective rate) covers `StripeFee`/`ApplicationFee` only. Orders enriched before these buckets were captured carry null for both (treated as zero) and are not re-enriched.
- VAT is computed locally per order using VIP-split logic on attendees with `Status` in (`Valid`, `CheckedIn`): ticket base = Σ min(price, 315) minus the order's `DiscountAmount` (floored at 0), VAT = base × 10/110 rounded once per order. Discount codes reduce the taxable ticket part, never the VIP donation. Orders not in `Paid` status carry `VatAmount = 0`. The vendor's tax line is intentionally ignored.
- Dashboard list and reporting pages render the section's service DTO rows directly; page models carry only paging/filter state and presentation metadata. The attendees view renders the VIP badge and split only above `TicketConstants.VipThresholdEuros`.
- All `/Tickets` dashboard aggregate metrics (`TicketsSold`, `Revenue`, `NetRevenue`, fee totals, `UnmatchedOrderCount`, the daily-sales chart, the per-payment-method fee breakdown) are computed only over orders with `PaymentStatus == Paid`; ticket counts within those are further restricted to attendees with `Status` in (`Valid`, `CheckedIn`). Refunded/Cancelled/Pending orders are still synced and visible on `/Tickets/Orders` (with the new Status column on Recent Orders) but never contribute to dashboard totals. The "unmatched orders" badge links to `/Tickets/Orders?filterMatched=false&filterPaymentStatus=Paid` so the count and the drill-down agree.
- The monthly accountant recap on `/Tickets/SalesAggregates` (and its `/Tickets/Export/AccountantReport` CSV) is the one exception to that: it buckets by purchase month in **Europe/Madrid**, and refunded orders contribute their original gross (`Refunded Gross`) and their Stripe/Ticket Tailor fees, which a refund does not return. `Ticket Income incl VAT` is built over the `Valid`/`CheckedIn` seats exactly as `ComputeOrderVat` builds the VAT base — Σ min(price, 315) less the order's `DiscountAmount`, floored at zero per order — so it equals the base `VatAmount` was charged on. The order gross cannot stand in for it: a transfer voids a seat and adds a live replacement at the same price without changing `TotalAmount`; `ex VAT` is that less the VAT. The derivation lives once in `TicketQueryService`; the view and the CSV only render it. Their grand totals share the monthly aggregate’s calculation, including fees on refunded orders, fee VAT and withheld tax.
- Ticket renderers own holdings ordering (non-void first, then attendee name ignoring case), team-filter names and the fee table (Stripe fees descending). Service holdings keep ownership filtering, while callers that only need counts avoid display sorting.
- Campaign average discounts derive from each aggregate’s total discount divided by paid redemptions, rounded to two decimals; an unused campaign reads zero. Attributed and vendor-only rows use the same calculation.
- The Sales Aggregates controller owns ticket-type ordering (face value descending) and campaign ordering (discount descending, then title ignoring case; unattributed vendor codes last). The query service computes their rows without display sorting.
- Weekly and quarterly rows share their paid-sales fields and net ticket revenue (gross less VAT, standalone donations and VIP donations). Their rows and footers use that same net calculation; it does not subtract processor fees.
- Sales aggregate reads return buckets without presentation sorting. The sales controller orders weeks by their start date, quarters by year/quarter, and months by ISO label; the accountant CSV applies the same month order.
- Monthly sales labels use Base’s invariant `yyyy-MM` formatter; purchase/refund bucketing stays in Europe/Madrid.
- Auto-matching uses normalized email comparison (`NormalizingEmailComparer`) against verified UserEmails rows only. Collisions among verified rows (a data-integrity error, should not happen) leave the email unmatched and emit `LogError`; nobody gets the ticket match. Buyer match writes `TicketOrder.MatchedUserId`; attendee match writes `TicketAttendee.MatchedUserId` independently.
- A user "has a ticket" iff at least one `Valid` or `CheckedIn` `TicketAttendee` is matched to their `UserId`. Buyer-only matches do not count — purchasing tickets for others does not give the buyer ticket coverage.
- On every **holder-facing** stub surface (homepage strip, holdings list) the Early Entry pill is the **viewer's own** earliest entry date, never the attendee's: both go through the single `TicketStubInfo.From(row, holderEarlyEntry)` mapper, which stamps one value the caller resolved from `IEarlyEntryService` for the *current* viewer. The pill can never be present on one holder surface and missing on another.
- The **Scanner gate card is the deliberate exception**: it is staff-facing and must show the *scanned attendee's* EE, not the operator's. `ScannerController` resolves `IEarlyEntryService` for `hit.MatchedUserId` and constructs `TicketStubInfo` directly instead of going through `From`. Do not "consolidate" it onto the shared mapper — that would blank the pill for gate staff.
- Attendee, order and donation CSV GETs retain browser cancellation through the internal service and decorator into repository reads. Actor resolution still runs first. An aborted read produces no completed-export audit; once rows are prepared, the existing audit remains independent of browser cancellation. CSV content and role gates are unchanged.
- Ticket-holdings reads retain caller cancellation through matched-attendee counts, the verified-email fallback (attendee emails and user index), and active-event settings used for the post-event deletion hold. Callers that supply no cancellation retain that behavior; these helpers perform no writes.
- Every stub shows the active event's configured `EventName` through `ISettingsService.GetActiveEventSettings`, falling back to the localized Tickets title when no event is configured. Its admission text, status badges and Early Entry label — like the holdings card's title, order count and empty state — use `TicketsResource` in all supported cultures.
- `TicketSyncState` is a singleton row (Id = 1). `LastSyncAt` is the resume cursor passed back to the vendor as `updated_at.gte` on the next run. A sync stuck in `Running` for >30 minutes is auto-reset to `Error` by `GetDashboardStatsAsync` (crash recovery).
- A vendor 5xx/transport failure **and** a vendor request timeout are both transient: `LastSyncAt` is preserved, `SyncStatus` returns to `Idle`, and the job retries next run without rethrowing. Requested shutdown cancellation propagates through vendor/Stripe/settings reads, logs Warning without an exception, and restores Idle with the original cursor and previous diagnostic using a non-cancellable save. Recovery covers cancellation reported before or after the Running/completion state save commits; the job rethrows cancellation without logging completion. A genuine fault, including cancellation while the caller token remains active, sets `SyncStatus = Error`, attempts to persist `LastError`, and rethrows. A failed error or cancellation-recovery save logs its own exception and event ID without replacing the original exception. TicketTailor refuses incomplete order totals/status or missing/non-EUR currency before any order upsert; these read refusals use the existing deferral path, preserving the cursor. The Warning records the reason as well as the HTTP status.
- Every configured sync attempt clears the local order and user-holdings cache slices on exit, including failure or cancellation: later stages can fail after order/attendee writes have committed, and readers must reload that persisted state.
- Sync failures persist an error summary within 2000 UTF-16 units without splitting a surrogate pair; the original exception is logged and rethrown in full.

## Negative Access Rules

- Board **cannot** trigger any sync (incremental or full), export CSV, or open the participation backfill page.
- A member **cannot** declare or withdraw `NotAttending` for anyone but themselves: both endpoints act on the signed-in user only, need the antiforgery token, and refuse when no active event year is set. A declaration never overrides an `Attended` record, and a withdrawal only clears the member's own `UserDeclared` `NotAttending`. A refused declaration displays the localized already-attended error; success feedback is shown only when the write succeeds.
- A non-admin viewer of someone else's profile **cannot** see the onsite chip (only Self or `TicketsPolicies.Read`).
- Board **cannot** trigger attendee contact import — same `TicketsPolicies.Manage` gate as the sync.
- Board **cannot** set or rotate the gate-terminal password (`SetPassword` is `TicketsPolicies.Manage`); it reads the credential status and the contacts preview (`TicketsPolicies.Read`).
- The gate-terminal account **cannot** reach any `/Tickets/*` page except the `ScannerAccess`-gated `/Tickets/Admin/Onsite` roster — it holds no roles, and no Tickets policy admits it.
- TicketsAdmin **cannot** trigger a Full Re-sync or open the participation backfill page (both `AdminOnly`).
- Nobody can edit ticket configuration (vendor `EventId`, API key, sync interval) from inside the app — those values come from `appsettings`'s `TicketVendor` section and the `TICKET_VENDOR_API_KEY` environment variable, set at deploy time.
- Regular humans have no access to `/Tickets/*` (dashboard, orders, attendees, codes, gate list, who-hasn't-bought, sales aggregates) — the controller-wide policy is `TicketsPolicies.Read`.
- A member **cannot** see another person's Humans-issued tickets: `/Tickets/Buy/Mine` reads the signed-in holder only, and the profile card renders nothing to a Public viewer (Self and Admin views only). The anonymous code image (`/Tickets/Code/{token}`) renders only a Data Protection token Humans minted for an issued barcode, never arbitrary text.
- Board **cannot** resend ticket emails (`Resend` is `TicketsPolicies.Manage`).
- TicketsAdmin **cannot** cancel an event (`PolicyNames.AdminOnly`) or use an override (both `PolicyNames.AdminOnly`). Board can open the cancel page and see its progress, never submit it.
- A cancelled event **cannot** change its ticketing settings, reopen resale, or restore a ticket.

## Triggers

- Delayed volunteer invitations require a resolved, non-tombstoned holder account. A stored ticket address cannot make a missing or tombstoned account eligible; a live merge uses the survivor’s email identity so preferences apply to that human.
- When a paid order's tickets are issued, checkout calls `ITicketDelivery.TicketsIssuedAsync`, which enqueues a Hangfire job (retried on failure) that sends a `ticket_issued` email per valid or offered ticket with an email, to its holder, carrying any child ticket without an email that rides on it (holder's language once signed in, else the order's; System category), a `ticket_order_receipt` to the buyer (order's language; System), both with a delivery id derived from the ticket or order so a repeated call reuses the outbox row; one `TicketDeliverySent` audit row counting the mails handed to Email and any ticket skipped for lack of an address; and a Hangfire job per non-child ticket two days later that sends `ticket_join_invitation` (VolunteerUpdates category, so the opt-out applies and is never locked) only if the ticket is still `Valid`, audited `TicketJoinInvitationSent`.

- `TicketSyncJob` stays internal to the section; registration and scheduling use its runtime type through `ISectionJobs`.
- Daily at 19:00 UTC (`tickets-resale-refund-batch`), `TicketResaleRefundJob` runs the evening resale refund batch (checkout.md) and audits its totals per event.
- Daily at 04:30 (`tickets-checkout-recon`), `TicketReconJob` reconciles every platform any event's ticketing settings use, from the first ticketing settings to the start of today: its orders, and its payment account's fees and payouts where Humans holds its Stripe read key (Box Office, pretix, Hi.Events). It logs an error for each check out of balance or unreadable and, when any is, queues one `ticket_reconciliation_alert` email (English, System) to the ticketing team's mailbox listing them, with a link to the reconciliation page for that range; it repeats nightly until everything balances. It changes nothing else.

- When ticket sync runs: vendor orders and issued tickets are upserted into `ticket_orders` / `ticket_attendees` (existing rows keyed by `VendorOrderId` / `VendorTicketId` retain their `Id` and their already-enriched fields; API-issued tickets (the retired transfer reissues) — null vendor order id — keep the locally-snapshotted `Price` so revenue/VAT stay anchored to the original order), gate check-ins pulled from the vendor's `/check_ins` are applied onto the attendee rows in a standalone pass keyed by `VendorTicketId` (`ITicketRepository.ApplyCheckInsAsync`, write-once `CheckedInAt`), Stripe fees are enriched for newly-paid orders, VAT is recomputed for every order, vendor discount codes are matched to `CampaignGrants` via `ICampaignWrite.MarkGrantsRedeemedAsync`, and event participation is reconciled. On success, `LastSyncAt` is set to the start-of-sync instant. The vendor caching decorator drops the event's cached vendor summary when the sync's order pull for that event succeeds, and the query caching decorator, which fronts internal `ITicketSyncService`, clears both tracked slices once the sync returns or throws: per-order `TicketOrderInfo` and per-user `UserTicketHoldings`. The order projection re-warms from the freshly-upserted rows on the next read; user holdings reload on demand and keep their 5-minute freshness window inside the tracked entry. Both per-user holdings and per-event vendor summary misses use the shared guarded loader, so reads begun before eviction cannot retain old data after it; expired entries still reload on demand.
- `EventParticipation` derivation (Ticket Tailor's active vendor event only, scoped to `EventSettings.Year` from `ISettingsService.GetActiveEventSettings`):
  - For each user with at least one matched attendee: any gate-checked-in ticket (`CheckedInAt` set — read from the persisted attendee rows, not inferred from `Status`, since TicketTailor keeps a scanned ticket `valid`) → `ParticipationStatus.Attended`, passing the earliest scan across the user's tickets down as the participation `CheckedInAt`; otherwise any `Valid` ticket → `ParticipationStatus.Ticketed`. Both write through the internal `IEventParticipationManagement.RecordParticipationAsync` with `ParticipationSource.TicketSync`.
  - For each prior `(TicketSync, Ticketed)` row in the year: if the user no longer has any `Valid`/`CheckedIn` matched ticket, the record is cleared via `IEventParticipationManagement.RemoveTicketSyncParticipationAsync`. `Attended` rows are never removed by sync — being checked in is permanent.
  - Self-declared `NotAttending` rows are the member's (`UserDeclared`), but a matched ticket overrides the declaration: a ticket is a physical thing, so sync flips a prior `NotAttending` to `Ticketed` (via the same `RecordParticipationAsync` path) when the user holds a `Valid`/`CheckedIn` ticket. Only `Attended` rows are never overwritten; the one way back is Gate undoing a mistaken admit (`ITicketWrite.UndoCheckInAsync`: Attended → Ticketed, `TicketSync`, `CheckedInAt` cleared), which the next sync converges.
- "Who Hasn't Bought" lists active Volunteers-team members (`ITeamService.GetTeamAsync(SystemTeamIds.Volunteers)`, active members only) minus those whose current-year `EventParticipation.Status` is `NotAttending`. `HasTicket` uses the has-a-ticket rule above, scoped to the current event (`TicketSyncState.VendorEventId`): a matched `Valid` or `CheckedIn` attendee row. Buyer-only, void and prior-event matches do not count.
- Code redemption: vendor discount codes attached to orders are pushed back to Campaigns via `ICampaignWrite.MarkGrantsRedeemedAsync` so each `CampaignGrant.RedeemedAt` reflects the order's `PurchasedAt`.
- Event participation reads (`ITicketService.GetParticipationsAsync`, forward-resolving a merged-away id; `GetAllParticipationsAsync`) are implemented by `TicketQueryService` through `IEventParticipationRepository`, remain uncached through the decorator, and use only `ticket_event_participations`. Historical null-status rows read as unknown; writes replace them, and removals delete records rather than writing cleared markers.
- When an account merge accepts, `EventParticipationService.ReassignAsync` (via `IUserMerge`) folds each source year into the target by precedence `Attended > Ticketed > NoShow > NotAttending` (the higher status brings its `Source` and `DeclaredAt`; the first `CheckedInAt` is kept), then deletes the source's records, including historical cleared rows. Retrying the fold leaves the same target records.
- Ticket ownership merges preserve order, attendee and transfer timestamps. Repository reassignment takes source/target IDs and cancellation; the shared merge callback retains its actor/time contract.
- When an account merge accepts, `TicketSyncService.ReassignAsync` re-FKs `TicketOrder.MatchedUserId`, `TicketAttendee.MatchedUserId`, and `TicketTransferRequest.SenderUserId` / `TicketTransferRequest.ReceiverUserId` from source to target. Order/attendee and transfer-row reassignment share one transaction, so a failed transfer update cannot leave ownership half moved; the caching decorator, which is the section's `IUserMerge` and forwards the fold to it, then drops the order projection and both users' `UserTicketHoldings` tracked entries. The per-user eviction is required: without it a merged user's homepage tickets card and holdings widget lag up to 5 minutes behind the fold. `AccountMergeService.MergeAsync` fans out across all `IUserMerge` registrations; the Tickets section participates through `CachingTicketQueryService`.
- On attendee contact import apply: for selected unmatched attendees, `MatchedUserId` is set (via `UpsertAttendeesAsync`), new users are provisioned (via `IAccountProvisioningService` with `ContactSource.TicketTailor`, Stub Profile + verified `UserEmail`), a single unverified row is replaced through Users' atomic provisioning operation, participation is written once per newly-matched user from the imported rows: any persisted `CheckedInAt` means `Attended` with the earliest imported scan, otherwise `Ticketed` (both `TicketSync` source), the caching decorator (which fronts `IAttendeeContactImportService`) clears both ticket slices once the apply returns or throws, and a single `TicketsAuditActions.TicketContactsImported` row records the summary.
- Attendance declarations retain request cancellation through actor resolution. Attendance active-year reads also retain it; caller cancellation propagates without failure feedback. The contact-import commit boundary is unchanged.
- Participation import, attendee/order/donation CSV exports and gate-password rotation resolve the current account through Users before proceeding. Merged sessions use the surviving account for audit and provisioning attribution; missing accounts retain Forbid for imports/exports and Challenge for password changes. Actor lookup cancellation propagates before any data read or mutation; existing operation tokens and import confirmation remain unchanged.
- Contact import preflight reads, including actor resolution, honour request cancellation. After preflight, provisioning, attendee links, participation and the summary audit finish independently of a closed tab; dependency failures retain the existing error/retry behaviour.
- Contact import skips multiple verified owners or multiple matching unverified rows (including aliases on one user). The preview marks them ambiguous with no selection checkbox; apply records them as skipped without deleting emails, provisioning users, or attaching tickets.
- Contact import groups addresses using Users' matching equivalence: case-insensitive, trimmed, and `gmail.com`/`googlemail.com` variants. Apply uses the same comparison when checking fresh group members, so one replacement/provisioning operation links the entire group. Gmail dots and `+tags` remain distinct, as in the existing Users lookup.
- On GDPR erasure (`IUserDataContributor.EraseForUserAsync`): buyer/attendee names and emails on the user's order and attendee rows, and the receiver snapshot on their transfer requests, are tombstoned in place (`ITicketRepository.EraseUserPiiAsync`, `ITicketTransferRepository.ErasePiiForUserAsync`); rows and vendor ids are kept for finance and sync reconciliation. Declared retention: sales-record. The `EventParticipations` slice is retained unchanged (no erase): the declaration is the one Users carried for its `event_participations` table, copied verbatim, whose erase path never touched those rows. The erasure is permanent across sync because the row's `PiiErasedAt` marker is stamped at erasure and `UpsertOrdersAsync`/`UpsertAttendeesAsync` skip the name/email fields (only those two) while it is set — every other field keeps syncing normally (nobodies-collective/Humans#1178). Rows erased before `PiiErasedAt` existed land with it `NULL`; the same guard also recognises the tombstone shape itself (`TicketPiiTombstone.IsTombstoned`) and self-heals the marker onto the row the first time a sync touches it.

- The AdminOnly participation CSV tool at `/Tickets/Participation/Backfill` first shows parsed valid rows, then requires an explicit confirm POST with an admin actor ID. `UserParticipationBackfillService` parses; `EventParticipationService` writes and logs one `ParticipationBackfilled` audit per changed member (entity `User`, the admin as actor) — a member listed twice reports once, with the committed status. Preview writes nothing and serializes the valid rows in input order through CsvHelper with the shared `HumansCsv` write configuration (CRLF records), ready for the confirmation POST. The result counts unique changed records, excluding protected Attended rows and no-op re-imports.

**Owning services (all in `Humans.Tickets.Services`, all `internal sealed`):**

The same owner assembles the two GDPR slices from matched orders and attendees, preserving identifiers, status values and ISO timestamps. The query service keeps the existing read and contributor contracts, slice-key aliases and sales-record erasure policy; its decorator retains cache ownership. **Vendor connectors — their own section, `Humans.TicketTailor`:**
Ticketing is three things, and conflating them is what a vendor change punishes. The **section**
(`Humans.Tickets`) is the application's only door to ticketing. The **port**
(`ITicketVendorService`, `Humans.Tickets/Contracts/`) is the vendor-agnostic contract, owned by
this section. The **adapter** (`Humans.TicketTailor`) is one implementation of it, and takes a direct
`ProjectReference` on `Humans.Tickets` to name the port. The port is deliberately **not** on the
`Humans.Tickets.Contracts` leaf: no Base consumer names it, and the leaf must keep the vendor's
vocabulary away from other sections (they use `ITicketDiscountCodes` / `ITicketVendorMirror`).
Retiring the vendor deletes the adapter project; nothing in `Humans.Tickets`, in Base or in any consumer changes.
Selling in Humans uses the second port, `ITicketingPlatform`, implemented by the BoxOffice, Pretix and HiEvents sections.

- `TicketTailorService` — production HTTP client; bound when the host environment is Production.
- `StubTicketVendorService` — deterministic in-memory fixture; bound everywhere else. The stub fills in placeholder `EventId`/`ApiKey` so `TicketVendorSettings.IsConfigured` returns true even without env vars.

`Section.Register` in the adapter reads the environment name out of `IConfiguration`
(`ISection.Register` has no `IHostEnvironment`) and fails closed: anything but an exactly-Production
environment name gets the stub, so a developer holding a real `TICKET_VENDOR_API_KEY` still cannot
write to a live ticketing account. The port's `IOptions<TicketVendorSettings>` binding lives in
Tickets' own `Section.Register` — the settings belong to the port, which `TicketSyncService` and the section's own
`TicketVendorHealthCheck` also read, and deleting the adapter must not take them with it.

**Only `Humans.Tickets` may inject `ITicketVendorService`:** the section's services and its own
`TicketVendorHealthCheck` (`Health/`, which probes the connector deliberately). Everything else asks Tickets,
through `Humans.Tickets.Contracts`. Pinned by
`tests/Humans.Web.Tests/Architecture/TicketVendorPortArchitectureTests.cs`.

**`TicketsGateAdminController` writes no Tickets table.** `/Tickets/Admin/Gate` drives this section's
`GateTerminalAccountSeeder` to set the shared gate terminal's password, and sits under this route prefix
because ticket admins are who rotate that credential. Whether it eventually lands in `Humans.Gate` is Gate's call.

**Stripe connector:** `IStripeService` (`Humans.Stripe`) wraps the Stripe SDK and is consumed by `TicketSyncService` for fee enrichment.

**Public surface:** the `Humans.Tickets.Contracts` leaf publishes `ITicketService`,
`ITicketDiscountCodes`, `ITicketVendorMirror`, `ITicketWrite`, `ITicketingPlatform` (the port each ticketing platform section implements), `ITicketingPlatformEvents` (platforms report a changed order) and `ITicketGateLookup` (Gate's read of Humans-issued tickets), with `EventParticipationInfo` and the participation enums, and `TicketsPlatformAdminNav`, which a ticketing platform section calls for its admin sidebar row so the route to Tickets' platform status page stays Tickets' own. The `TicketDashboardDtos` surface is internal.
Tickets ships both a `.Contracts` leaf *and* a `Contracts/` folder: the
leaf carries what cross-section consumers need, the folder carries public surface that is ASP.NET plumbing
(`TicketStubViewComponent`) or the vendor port. The split is a judgement about what cross-section consumers should
have to see, **not** a compiler constraint: every leaf referencing `Humans.Base` resolves `Microsoft.AspNetCore.App` transitively. All section services route every database
read/write through `ITicketRepository` / `ITicketTransferRepository` / `IEventParticipationRepository` / `ITicketCheckoutRepository`; no service imports `Microsoft.EntityFrameworkCore` or references `TicketsDbContext`.

Issued-ticket sync never invents a valid attendee status when the vendor omits it: the complete read fails before attendee writes.

Vendor event summaries require a readable response with a nonblank name and an explicit sold count. Incomplete TicketTailor responses fail instead of entering the 15-minute summary cache as an unnamed or zero-sold event; explicit zero remains valid.

**Caching:** `CachingTicketQueryService` (`Services/Stores/`, Singleton — `ApplicationServicesTakeNoMemoryCacheRule` sweeps `Humans.*.Services` and would flag it in `Services/`) wraps a keyed-inner `TicketQueryService` (Scoped) and composes two tracked slices: `OrdersCache : TrackedCache<Guid, TicketOrderInfo>` (with `warmOnStartup: true`) keyed by ticket order id, and `UserHoldingsCache : TrackedCache<Guid, CachedUserTicketHoldings>` keyed by user id. The order slice owns the per-order `TicketOrderInfo` projection with attendees embedded; the user slice owns `UserTicketHoldings` and stores the 5-minute freshness deadline in the tracked value. The warm path calls the normal `ITicketManagement.GetTicketOrdersAsync` read method on the keyed inner, not a cache-named helper. Composition (multiple inner caches with different shapes) precludes inheriting `TrackedCache` directly, so the decorator implements `IHostedService` itself and forwards `StartAsync` to both tracked caches; the user slice has `warmOnStartup: false`. It takes no `IMemoryCache` of its own and has no seam onto the vendor summary cache, which belongs to `CachingTicketVendorService` below. The cached rows are written by three inner services, so the decorator also fronts their interfaces (`ITicketSyncService`, `IAttendeeContactImportService`) and is the section's `IUserDataContributor` and `IUserMerge`; each inner is keyed under the same `InnerServiceKey`, and every write pass-through evicts in a `finally`. The one invalidation seam outside the decorator is `InvalidateHoldings`: 2027 ticketing (`TicketCheckoutService`) calls it for each person who gains a Humans-issued ticket (issued on payment, a companion, a rename), because those tickets feed the holdings' deletion hold and a deletion falling due inside the TTL must see them.

`CachingTicketVendorService` (`Services/Stores/`, Singleton) is the caching decorator over `ITicketVendorService` — the port itself, not the query surface above. It caches only `GetEventSummaryAsync`, in a `TrackedCache<string, CachedVendorEventSummary>` keyed by vendor event id with a 15-minute freshness deadline stored in the tracked value. Every other port member forwards straight through, uncached. It lives beside the port in Tickets (not the adapter) so a 2027 vendor swap doesn't touch it; `Humans.Tickets`' own `Section.cs` registers it (and the keyed inner it wraps, bound by whichever vendor adapter section is registered — Tickets never names it). It evicts an event's summary on its own `GetOrdersAsync` pass-through, after a successful pull: the ticket sync is that member's only caller, so the summary is never older than the orders last synced.

### Repositories

- **`ITicketRepository`** (Tickets-owned) — owns reads/writes for `ticket_orders`, `ticket_attendees`, `ticket_sync_states`, `ticket_settings`. Aggregate-local navs kept (`TicketOrder.Attendees`, `TicketAttendee.TicketOrder`). Cross-domain `MatchedUser` nav properties have been stripped from both entities; FK (`MatchedUserId`) is retained; grid views render it through `<vc:human>` without copying a user name into the row.
- **`ITicketTransferRepository`** (Tickets-owned) — owns `ticket_transfer_requests`: the approved-row read for the order projection, merge reassignment and GDPR erasure. Nothing creates a request.
- **`IEventParticipationRepository`** (Tickets-owned, Singleton) — owns reads/writes for `ticket_event_participations`, including the lifecycle rules and the merge fold.

The Tickets→Budget bridge is Budget's: `Humans.Budget.Services.TicketingBudgetService` reads paid-order data through `ITicketService` and delegates all writes to Budget's `IBudgetManagement`; its tests live at `tests/Humans.Budget.Tests/Services/TicketingBudgetServiceTests.cs`.

### Touch-and-clean guidance

- New cross-section data needs always go through the owning section's interface — `ICampaignService` / `ICampaignWrite`, `IUserService` / `IUserWrite`, `IUserEmailService`, `ITeamService`, `ISettingsService`, `IBudgetService`, `ICampService`, `ISystemRoleService`, `IEarlyEntryService`. The `MatchedUser` nav properties have been stripped from both entities; do not re-add them. Pass `MatchedUserId` to `<vc:human>` for display.
- No Tickets service holds an `IMemoryCache` directly; the per-event `Tickets.VendorEventSummary` slice and both tracked ticket slices live in `TrackedCache` instances on the two caching decorators. The inner services (`TicketQueryService`, `TicketSyncService`, `AttendeeContactImportService`) are cache-free and never evict; each decorator evicts its own slices on its own pass-throughs. A new service that writes cached ticket rows becomes another keyed inner the query decorator fronts, never a caller of an eviction seam. Do not push `IMemoryCache` into controllers, view components, or other domain services.
- `CachingTicketQueryService.GetDashboardStatsAsync` is a pass-through to the inner, so dashboard statistics are computed on each render; on-demand staleness during sync windows is currently acceptable.
- When extending the Tickets→Budget bridge, remember it lives in Budget: source new read data from `ITicketService` (adding methods there only if the existing `GetTicketOrdersAsync` read model is insufficient), and edit `TicketingBudgetService` in `src/Sections/Humans.Budget/Services/`. Projection/line-item writes stay Budget-owned.
- The vendor split is doctrinal: business code talks to `ITicketVendorService` and never to "Ticket Tailor" directly. Any new vendor capability needs an interface method first, then a `TicketTailorService` impl plus a deterministic `StubTicketVendorService` impl so dev/preview environments still exercise the call.

## Issue queue

`Section` also declares this queue’s Area-label resource key and page-path aliases; Issues discovers both with the queue.

Tickets owns the `Tickets` issue queue: it implements `IIssueQueueOwner` (Issues' `Contracts/`
folder) on its `Section` entry point, declaring the queue key and the roles that handle
issues filed against it — `TicketsAdmin`, plus `Admin`, which handles every queue. Issues
discovers the declaration through DI and holds no list of sections; dropping the seam
sends this section's stored issues to the Admin-only queue.
